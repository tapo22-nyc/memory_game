============================================================
WHAT ARE WE TRYING TO DO — THE BIG PICTURE
============================================================

Every player in our fantasy game needs two numbers:

  1. IMPACT SCORE (0 to 100)
     How valuable is this player in a match?
     Higher = better fantasy pick.

  2. PLAYER BUDGET ($50,000 to $250,000)
     How expensive is this player to pick in your team?
     Better players cost more. Total team budget = $1,000,000
     across 11 players.

The challenge is that raw career stats (e.g. Virat Kohli has
27,000+ international runs, Lahiru Kumara has 62 ODI runs)
are on completely different scales. We can't just add them up.
So we first NORMALISE every stat to a 0–100 scale before
calculating scores. This is what the 3 queries do step by step.


============================================================
QUERY 1 — WHAT ARE THE NORMALIZED COMPONENT SCORES?
============================================================

PURPOSE:
  Break each player's raw career stats into individual
  "component scores" — each between 0 and 100 — so that
  all stats are on a fair, comparable scale.

HOW IT WORKS:

  Step 1 — Assign a role to each player.
  The query looks up each player's role (Batter, Bowler,
  All Rounder, Wicketkeeper) from our cricket_player_pool
  table. Players not in that table (most IPL players) get
  role = "unknown" because cricapi_player_master has no
  player_type column yet.

  Step 2 — Unpivot the data.
  Our career stats table has one wide row per player with
  columns for ODI, T20, and Test all in one row. The query
  splits this into 3 separate rows per player — one for T20,
  one for ODI, one for Test. So a player like Virat Kohli
  appears 3 times in the output.

  Step 3 — Normalize using PERCENT_RANK.
  For each stat (e.g. runs), we rank every player against
  all other players IN THE SAME FORMAT. The player with the
  most runs gets 100. The player with the least gets 0.
  Everyone else is proportionally in between.

  Example:
    Virat Kohli has the most Test runs → runs_score = 100
    A player with 0 Test runs → runs_score = 0
    Someone in the middle → runs_score = ~50

  IMPORTANT — Economy rate is INVERTED:
    Lower economy = better bowler. So we flip it.
    Economy rate of 6.0 (great) gets a HIGH economy_score.
    Economy rate of 10.0 (terrible) gets a LOW economy_score.

COMPONENT SCORES PRODUCED:
  Batting:
    runs_score          — how many total runs vs all players
    batting_avg_score   — batting average vs all players
    strike_rate_score   — strike rate vs all players
    fifties_score       — number of 50s vs all players
    hundreds_score      — number of 100s vs all players
    boundary_score      — 4s + weighted 6s vs all players
    sixes_score         — raw sixes vs all players
    bat_experience_score— matches played as batter

  Bowling:
    wickets_score       — total wickets vs all players
    economy_score       — economy rate (inverted, lower = better)
    five_wicket_score   — 5-wicket hauls vs all players
    ten_wicket_score    — 10-wicket match hauls (Test only)
    bowl_experience_score— matches played as bowler

OUTPUT:
  One row per player per format (3 rows per player).
  Shows the raw stat AND the normalized score side by side
  so you can verify the normalization looks sensible.


============================================================
QUERY 2 — WHAT IS THE FINAL IMPACT SCORE AND BUDGET?
============================================================

PURPOSE:
  Take all the component scores from Query 1 and combine
  them into a single Impact Score and a single Budget number
  for each player in each format.

HOW IT WORKS:

  Step 1 — Calculate a BATTING IMPACT SCORE per format.
  Combines batting component scores using weights:

    T20:  Runs (30%) + Strike Rate (30%) + Boundaries (20%) + Sixes (20%)
    ODI:  Runs (40%) + Batting Avg (30%) + Strike Rate (30%)
    Test: 100s (40%) + 50s (30%) + Runs (30%)

  The logic: in T20, how fast you score and how many
  boundaries you hit matters as much as total runs. In Tests,
  centuries and fifties signal quality more than sheer volume.

  Step 2 — Calculate a BOWLING IMPACT SCORE per format.
  Combines bowling component scores using weights:

    T20:  Wickets (50%) + Economy (50%)
    ODI:  Wickets (55%) + Economy (45%)
    Test: Wickets (40%) + 5-wicket hauls (35%) + 10-wicket hauls (25%)

  The logic: in T20, economy matters as much as wickets.
  In Tests, 5-wicket and 10-wicket hauls are the mark of
  a great bowler.

  Step 3 — Combine batting + bowling by ROLE.
  This is where the player's role is used:

    Batter / Wicketkeeper → Final Impact = Batting Impact only
    Bowler               → Final Impact = Bowling Impact only
    All Rounder          → Final Impact = 50% Batting + 50% Bowling
    Unknown              → Final Impact = average of both (fallback)

  Step 4 — Convert to Budget ($50k–$250k).
  The same role-weighted score is used as the budget input.
  It is then scaled linearly:
    Score of 0   → Budget = $50,000
    Score of 100 → Budget = $250,000
    Score of 50  → Budget = $150,000
  Budget is rounded to the nearest $5,000.

OUTPUT:
  One row per player per format.
  Shows: batting_impact_score, bowling_impact_score,
         final_impact_score, raw_budget_score, final_player_budget.


============================================================
QUERY 3 — DISTRIBUTION CHECK
============================================================

PURPOSE:
  A summary view to check whether the scores and budgets
  produced by Query 2 are spread out sensibly across all
  players. This is a validation step — not used in the app.

HOW IT WORKS:
  Groups all players by FORMAT and ROLE, then shows:
    - How many players are in each group
    - Min, P25, Median, P75, Max of impact scores
    - Min, P25, Median, P75, Max of budgets

  P25 = bottom 25% of players
  Median = middle player (50th percentile)
  P75 = top 25% of players

WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE:
  Impact median around 40–60 per group (well spread)
  Budget median around $130k–$160k (middle of the range)
  P25 budget above $80k (even weak players aren't dirt cheap)
  P75 budget below $220k (stars expensive but not always $250k)

WHAT WE SAW IN THE OUTPUT:
  - "unknown" role has a very high player count — this is
    because most IPL players have no player_type in
    cricapi_player_master. They are all being scored as
    "unknown" and getting an average of batting + bowling.
    This needs to be fixed before scores are meaningful
    for IPL players.
  - SL/WI players (batter, bowler, all_rounder) are scoring
    correctly because their roles ARE in cricket_player_pool.


============================================================
KNOWN ISSUE — VIRAT KOHLI SCORES LOWER THAN ALL-ROUNDERS
============================================================

This is a real problem with the current formula and it is
worth fixing before deploying.

WHY IT HAPPENS:
  Virat Kohli is a "batter". His final impact score is based
  on his BATTING impact only.

  All-rounders like Kyle Jamieson and Jason Holder get
  BOTH their batting AND bowling scores averaged together.
  If Jason Holder has a moderate bowling impact (say 65)
  and a moderate batting impact (say 45), his all-rounder
  score is 55. Virat's pure batting impact in Test might
  also calculate to around 55 due to the normalization
  pool including ALL 149 players — many of whom are heavy
  run-scorers too.

ROOT CAUSES:
  1. The normalization pool includes ALL 149 players across
     all roles. Batters are being ranked against bowlers who
     also bat. This pulls elite batters down.
  2. All-rounders get credit for BOTH disciplines. A decent
     all-rounder with mediocre batting AND mediocre bowling
     can outscore a great specialist batter whose bowling
     contribution is zero.
  3. Test format has hundreds_score weighted at 40%, but
     because so many players in the pool have 0 Test 100s,
     even players with 5–10 hundreds cluster together near
     the top — the differentiation is lost.

HOW TO FIX (to discuss in next session):
  Option A — Normalize within role groups, not globally.
    Rank batters only against other batters, bowlers only
    against other bowlers. This way Virat is ranked against
    Rohit, Root, Warner — not against Bumrah.

  Option B — Add a floor boost for elite players.
    Players with Test average > 50 or 10,000+ runs get a
    bonus score added on top. This is a manual tier system.

  Option C — Separate "volume" from "quality" stats.
    Weight batting average and strike rate more heavily
    than raw runs. Virat's avg of 48+ is elite. Raw runs
    inflate players who just played more games.

  Option D — Give all-rounders a slight penalty.
    Instead of 50/50 split, use 45% batting + 45% bowling
    with a 10% cap — so they never exceed what a true
    specialist would score at peak.

The right fix is most likely Option A combined with C.
We will address this in the next session before deploying
the formula into the app.
